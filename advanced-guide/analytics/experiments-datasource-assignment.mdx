description: 'Configure CRM-driven Experiment assignment and understand its statistical implications'

Datasource assignment evaluates CRM conditions to assign customers to Experiment Variants without using random assignment in the Experiment.

## Prerequisites

- A live CRM Datasource must contain the customer identifier used by the Workflow.
- The field used by the conditions must contain values assigned at random for causal measurement.
- Datasource assignment is available for Global holdouts and Continuous A/B tests, with **Per customer** randomization.

For UI instructions, see [Assign Variants from a Datasource](/product-guide/analytics/experiments-datasource-assignment).

## Evaluation

Conditions are evaluated in the order configured, independently of the Variant order. The first condition met determines the customer's Variant.

Each Variant requires a condition with at least one rule. Rules within a condition can use **and/or** logic, as in a Condition Node.

There is no catch-all condition for all other Datasource rows. Write a condition for every group you want to classify explicitly.

Date and datetime fields do not offer date-range operators in the condition editor.

## Worked example

Suppose a CRM field named `holdout_group` contains `control` or `treatment`, assigned at random. The Experiment is a Global holdout with an expected 90/10 allocation.

| Condition | Variant |
|-----------|---------|
| `holdout_group = control` | Control |
| `holdout_group = treatment` | Treatment |

A row with an empty `holdout_group` matches neither condition. The customer receives the Treatment fallback and is excluded from results.

An unknown customer has no CRM row, so they also receive the Treatment fallback and are excluded from results.

The configured 90/10 allocation is an expectation, not an assignment rule. The CRM field determines which Variant each classified customer receives.

## Unclassified customers

Customers are unclassified when they meet no condition, have no CRM row, or are anonymous. They receive the fallback and are not counted in the results.

| Experiment type | Fallback Variant |
|-----------------|------------------|
| Global holdout | Treatment |
| Continuous A/B test | Reference |

## Causal validity

Random assignment in the Experiment is the only method that guarantees a causal reading. Datasource assignment supports causal measurement only when the condition values were themselves assigned at random.

If a field reflects an existing segment, selection bias means observed differences cannot be attributed to the Experiment's Variants. Examples include opt-outs, a hand-picked population, a behavioural cohort, or a region.

Observed counts per Variant drive the statistical intervals, not the expected allocations. A small Control produces wider confidence intervals and lower statistical power.

For Experiments with more than two Variants, every group must be randomised.

## Editing a live Experiment

The Datasource, conditions, and their order remain editable after start, including while the Experiment is **Maturing**. Changes reach new traffic within about a minute.

Customers who move between Variants because of an edit are excluded from results. You are responsible for accounting for this attrition when interpreting results.

While an Experiment is **Maturing**, every new customer receives the fallback, regardless of the conditions. The assignment method itself cannot be changed after start.

<Info>
  Negated operators such as **is not equal** and **does not contain** also match empty or missing values, as they do in the Condition Node.
</Info>
