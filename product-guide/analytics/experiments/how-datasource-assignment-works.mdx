---
title: 'How Datasource assignment works'
description: 'Understand how a CRM Datasource assigns Variants and how to read the results'
---

Datasource assignment uses rules on your CRM [Datasource](/glossary) to give each customer a Variant, instead of a random draw.

## Before you begin

- You need a published CRM Datasource that contains the customer identifier used by your Workflow.
- Datasource assignment works with Global holdouts and Continuous A/B tests, with **Randomization unit** set to **Per customer**.
- To measure the effect of the Variants, use a field whose values were assigned at random and do not change during the Experiment.

For setup steps, see [Assign Variants from a Datasource](/product-guide/analytics/experiments/datasource-assignment).

## How conditions are checked

Conditions are checked in the order you set, whatever the order of the Variants. Each customer gets the Variant of the first condition they meet.

Each Variant needs a condition with at least one rule. Rules in a condition can use **and/or** logic, as in a [Condition Node](/product-guide/workflows/logic-nodes/conditions).

<Info>
  There is no catch-all condition for the other Datasource rows. Write a condition for every group you want to classify.
</Info>

Date and date-time fields do not offer date-range operators in the condition editor.

## Example

A CRM field named `holdout_group` contains `control` or `treatment`, assigned at random. The Experiment is a Global holdout with an expected Control allocation of 10%.

| Condition | Variant |
|-----------|---------|
| `holdout_group = control` | Control |
| `holdout_group = treatment` | Treatment |

<Info>
  A row with an empty `holdout_group` meets neither condition. The customer receives the Treatment and is left out of the results.
</Info>

The 10% expected allocation is only an estimate. The CRM field decides which Variant each classified customer receives.

## Unclassified customers

A customer is unclassified when they meet no condition, have no CRM row, are anonymous, or their CRM row cannot be read. They receive a fallback Variant and are not counted in the results.

| Experiment type | Fallback Variant |
|-----------------|------------------|
| Global holdout | Treatment |
| Continuous A/B test | Reference |

Results describe classified customers only.

## When results measure the Variants' effect

Only random assignment in the Experiment guarantees that results measure the effect of the Variants. With a Datasource, this holds only when the condition values were themselves assigned at random.

If a field reflects an existing segment, results show differences between segments, not the effect of the Variants. Examples include opt-outs, hand-picked groups, behavioural cohorts or regions.

Every group must come from the same random draw. Any other rule must be the same in every condition and combined with **and**. For example, adding `country = FR` to `holdout_group = control` only breaks the comparison.

Results use the number of customers actually seen in each Variant, not the expected allocation. When statistical significance is shown, a small Control takes longer to reach it.

## Edit while live

The Datasource, conditions, and their order stay editable until the Experiment is concluded. Changes reach new traffic within about a minute.

A customer's Variant changes when you edit the conditions or when their CRM values change. In [Data Exploration](/product-guide/analytics/data-exploration), that customer appears under each Variant they received, which blurs the difference between Variants.

Moves also bias the comparison when they depend on the Variant a customer received. Avoid moving classified customers after start.

You cannot switch between random and Datasource assignment once the Experiment has started.
