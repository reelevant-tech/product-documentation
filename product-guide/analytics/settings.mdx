## Overview

The Analytics Settings page lets you configure attribution, personalisation exposure, custom tags and branch categories for your company. Use tags and categories to qualify Branches and segment Analytics.

Access it from the **Settings** link in the Analytics sidebar navigation.

## Attribution

Attribution shows how much revenue your Reelevant [Workflows](/product-guide/workflows/overview) helped generate. Choose the settings that match your purchase data and reporting goals.

![Attribution configuration on the General tab: dataset, attribution source, model, method, and conversion channels with lookback windows](/images/analytics/attribution-settings-reelevant.png)

### Dataset and attribution source

Start with **Dataset**, then choose an **Attribution source**. UTM parameters are available only with Web tracking because customer purchase records have no URL.

| Setting | Options and behaviour | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Dataset** | **Web tracking** records purchases tracked by Reelevant. **Customer purchases** uses purchase records supplied by your business. | A web-only brand uses Web tracking. A retailer with store and web sales in its CRM or ERP uses Customer purchases. |
| **Attribution source** | **Reelevant interactions** credits qualifying interactions with Reelevant Content. **UTM parameters** measures UTM-tagged marketing visits and is available only with Web tracking. | Choose Reelevant interactions to isolate Reelevant's contribution. Choose UTM parameters to compare paid search, email, and affiliate sources. |

### Attribution model

The model decides how credit is shared between qualifying interactions before a purchase.

| Model | How credit is shared | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Last touch** (default) | The last qualifying interaction receives all the credit. | Choose it when you want results comparable with classic last-click reporting. |
| **Linear** | Every qualifying interaction receives an equal share. | Choose it when a fashion shopper's email, onsite, and app interactions all matter. |
| **Time decay** | Interactions closer to the purchase receive more credit, while earlier assists still receive some. | Choose it when a subscription brand wants recent interactions to count more without removing earlier assists. |
| **Incremental** | Credit is proportional to the measured Uplift of each Channel (for example, email versus onsite) against a modelled non-personalised baseline. Shares are rescaled to the purchase total. This is not a randomised holdout. | Choose it to compare measured Uplift from email and onsite Channels, using the Uplift dashboard's measurement. |

### Attribution method

| Method | How it works | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Default** | Credits any qualifying interaction from the customer. | Choose it when any relevant interaction may have influenced the purchase. |
| **Same reference ID** | Credits an interaction only when the purchased product's reference ID matches a product shown in the Content. Product IDs come from the Output Node's Datasource metadata. | Choose it for product recommendation Workflows when you only want credit for products the customer bought after seeing them recommended. |

### Purchase time precision

This setting appears only with **Customer purchases**.

| Option | How it works | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Date and time** (default) | An interaction must happen before the purchase timestamp. Web tracking always uses this precision. | Keep it when purchase records contain real times. It avoids crediting an afternoon click after a morning purchase. |
| **Date only** | Treats the purchase as a calendar day. Interactions through the end of that day can qualify, while the lookback window still starts at midnight. | Choose it for store receipts exported with dates only, where every purchase time is midnight. |

### Conversion channels

Conversion channels are available with **Reelevant interactions**. Each channel has a **Channel key** and **Interactions**, with an interaction type and **Lookback window (hours)**.

**Web tracking** uses one fixed `web` channel. With **Customer purchases**, each Channel key matches the purchase's transaction source. Purchases without a matching key are excluded unless **Any other source (*)** is configured; it claims every source without its own channel.

| Setting | Options and behaviour | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Channel key** | Matches the purchase transaction source. **Any other source (*)** catches sources without a matching channel. | Set `store` and `web` separately when their buyer journeys differ. Add `*` if new sources such as a marketplace must not be dropped. |
| **Interactions** | Add **Click** and/or **Display** for each channel. Defaults are 720 hours (30 days) for Click and 168 hours (7 days) for Display. | An email-heavy brand can add Display to count opens; Apple Mail privacy opens also count as displays, so this is generous. |
| **Lookback window (hours)** | Sets how long each interaction type can earn credit before the purchase. | Use Click and Display with a 48-hour window for store buyers who rarely click. Use a 720-hour Click window for web buyers. |

### UTM configuration

When **UTM parameters** is selected, choose the UTM grain and lookback window.

| Setting | Options and behaviour | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **UTM grain** | Choose Source, Medium, Campaign, Term, and/or Content. Source and Medium are selected by default. | Keep Source and Medium for a channel-level view. Add Campaign to compare campaigns. |
| **Lookback window (hours)** | Defaults to 720 hours. | Keep 720 hours when campaigns may influence purchases over 30 days. |

### Conversion funnel

The optional funnel contains ordered **Step name** and **Events** entries. Its last step is the conversion.

| Setting | How it works | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Conversion funnel** | Add the events that mark each stage, in order. | A lead-generation brand can use `signup_start` followed by `signup_complete`. |

### Configuring attribution

1. Open **Analytics > Settings**, then select the **General** tab.
2. Choose a **Dataset** and **Attribution source**.
3. Choose your **Attribution model** and **Attribution method**.
4. Configure conversion channels, UTM settings, or a conversion funnel when available.
5. For Customer purchases, choose **Purchase time precision**.
6. Click **Save changes**.

<Info>
  Changing attribution affects the [Conversions dashboard](/product-guide/analytics/dashboards/conversions) and [Data Exploration](/product-guide/analytics/data-exploration). A longer window may credit more interactions.
</Info>

## Customer lifecycle

Customer lifecycle settings define purchase thresholds and the periods used to classify customers. Leave a field empty to use its default.

Stages include Prospect, One-time buyer, Repeater, Loyal, At risk, and Churned.

| Setting | Default | What it controls | Example |
|---|---:|---|---|
| **Active window (days)** | 180 | The period used to identify active customers. | A furniture retailer with annual purchases might use 365 days; a grocery replenishment brand might use 90. |
| **Churn window (days)** | 365 | Past this period without a purchase, a customer is churned; between the active and churn windows, they are at risk. | A furniture retailer might use 730 days; a replenishment brand might use 180. |
| **Repeater purchases** | 2 | Purchases needed for the repeater stage. | Keep 2 when a second grocery or beauty purchase shows a repeat buying pattern. |
| **Loyal purchases** | 5 | Purchases needed for the loyal stage. | A beauty brand can raise this threshold so loyalty means several replenishment purchases. |
| **History window (days)** | 730 | How far back Analytics reads purchases. | Use 180 days when a recently re-ingested dataset contains only six months of history. |
| **Exposure window (days)** | 90 | The period over which a customer counts as exposed to Reelevant. | A brand with a longer buying cycle might use 180 days so earlier personalised interactions remain in scope. |

## Reporting

**TCO (total cost of ownership)** is the monthly Reelevant platform cost. Analytics prorates it over the selected period for cost and ROI measures.

| Setting | What it controls | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Total cost of ownership (TCO)** | Monthly cost used by cost and ROI measures. Cost and ROI measures need it to be set. | Enter your monthly Reelevant cost to compare report revenue with the real platform cost. |

## Automatic reports

Use the **Automatic reports** tab to configure recurring period reports. See the [Reports guide](/product-guide/analytics/reports) and the [advanced guide](/advanced-guide/analytics/reports).

## Personalisation exposure

These two thresholds decide which customers count as personalised in the [Customer Impact](/advanced-guide/analytics/customer-impact) dashboard. That dashboard and [Adoption](/advanced-guide/analytics/adoption) are both in beta and enabled on request, so you may not see them yet.

![Personalization exposure settings with the minimum personalised displays and minimum personalised share fields](/images/analytics/analytics-settings-personalisation-exposure.png)

| Field | Default | What it does | Example |
|---|---:|---|---|
| **Minimum personalized displays** | 2 | How many personalised Contents a customer must have seen. | A high-volume retailer can raise this to require several displays before counting a customer as personalised. |
| **Minimum personalized share (%)** | 50 | How much of what they saw must be personalised. | Raise it when your company personalises most of its Content and you want a more consistently personalised group. |

Customers above zero but below these thresholds count as neither personalised nor non-personalised. They stay out of the comparison.

1. Navigate to **Analytics > Settings**.
2. Scroll to **Personalization exposure** on the **General** tab.
3. Set the two thresholds, or leave them empty to keep the defaults.
4. Click **Save changes**.

<Tip>
  For a company that personalises most of its Content, raise **Minimum personalized share (%)**. Lower it when personalisation is limited to a few Contents.
</Tip>

## Comparison reliability

This threshold decides when the [Customer Impact](/advanced-guide/analytics/customer-impact) dashboard shows an Uplift figure, and when it leaves it empty.

| Field | Default | What it does | Example |
|---|---:|---|---|
| **Minimum customers per cohort** | 100 | How many customers each side of the comparison must hold. | A large retailer can raise it to reduce noisy Uplift figures. A small B2B company can lower it to keep smaller cohorts, accepting less reliable comparisons. |

Both sides need that many customers: the personalised ones and the comparable non-personalised ones. Below it, the dashboard shows the customer counts but no figure.

An average over a handful of customers moves with a single basket. So an empty figure means "we cannot answer this", not "no effect".

1. Navigate to **Analytics > Settings**.
2. Scroll to **Comparison reliability** on the **General** tab.
3. Set the threshold, or leave it empty to keep the default.
4. Click **Save changes**.

<Info>
  Customer Impact also needs a period of at least one month. Over a shorter one, being reached by Reelevant and being active are almost the same thing. The dashboard opens on the last three months for that reason.
</Info>

## Tags

Tags give each Branch controlled labels that your team can use consistently across Workflows and Analytics.

Each tag contains a **tag key** (the label to organise) and a list of **values**. For example, `Campaign objective` can contain `Acquisition` and `Retention`.

Only custom tags appear in this tab. Reelevant-managed tags are not editable.

### Understand cardinality

Cardinality controls how many values from one tag key can be assigned to the same Branch. It does not limit how many values you create.

![New tag key drawer showing the Single and Multiple cardinality choices](/images/analytics/tag-cardinality.png)

| Cardinality | Behaviour in a Workflow | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Single** | Each final Branch resolves to one value from this tag key. | `Campaign objective: Retention` cannot be combined with `Campaign objective: Acquisition` on the same Branch. |
| **Multiple** | Each final Branch keeps every value assigned along it. | `Audience context: Birthday` and `Audience context: Loyalty` can both describe the same Branch. |

**Most downstream** means closest to the [Output Node](/glossary).

Imagine one Workflow qualifies `Campaign objective: Acquisition` before a second Logic Node.

A later qualification on the same final Branch assigns `Campaign objective: Retention`. Analytics uses `Retention` because it is closer to the Output Node.

With **Multiple** cardinality, both values remain attached to the final Branch.

<Tip>
  Choose **Single** when one downstream value should replace earlier values. Choose **Multiple** when every matching value should remain available.
</Tip>

### Create a tag key

1. Navigate to **Analytics > Settings**.
2. Select **Tags**.
3. Click **Add tag**.
4. Enter a unique **Name**.
5. Add an optional **Description** to help teammates use the tag correctly.
6. Choose **Single** or **Multiple** under **Cardinality**.
7. Click **Create tag key**.

Tag key names must be unique. The platform ignores capitalisation and leading or trailing spaces when checking names.

### Add values

1. Find the tag key.
2. Click the plus icon beside **Values**.
3. Enter a unique value name and an optional description.
4. Click **Add value**.

Value names must be unique within their tag key. You can expand **Values** to review every available choice.

The platform also ignores capitalisation and leading or trailing spaces when checking value names.

### Edit or delete tags

Use the pencil icon to change a tag key, its description, or its cardinality.

Expand **Values**, then use each value's action icons to edit or delete it.

<Warning>
  Deleting a tag key also deletes all its values. Deleted tag keys and values cannot be restored.
</Warning>

### Use tags in Workflows and Analytics

Created tags become available when you [qualify a Branch](/product-guide/workflows/branches). Users select controlled values but cannot create new ones there.

In Analytics, use tag keys and values to filter results or compare performance across your qualification choices.

## Branch categories

![Branch categories settings tab showing the category tree](/images/workflows/branch-categories-settings-v2.png)

Branch categories are a hierarchical tree of categories used to **qualify Branches** across all your Workflows. They give your team a shared vocabulary and power Analytics filtering by category.

Open the **Branch categories** tab in Analytics Settings to manage them alongside your tags.

### Viewing the category tree

The tab displays your category tree as an interactive, expandable hierarchy. Each category shows:
- **Name** — The category label.
- **Level** — Its depth in the tree.
- **Children** — Sub-categories nested underneath.

### Creating a category

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the form">
    Click the **Add** button to open the category creation drawer.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Fill in the details">
    - **Name** — A descriptive name for the category (e.g., "Product Recommendation").
    - **Parent** — Optionally select a parent category to nest this one underneath.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Save">
    Click **Save** to add the category to the tree.
  </Step>
</Steps>

### Editing a category

Click on an existing category in the tree to open the edit drawer. You can update the name or move it under a different parent.

### Deleting a category

<Warning>
  Deleting a category removes it from all Workflows where it is used. Branches that referenced the deleted category will lose their qualification.
</Warning>

1. Select the category in the tree.
2. Click the **Delete** button.
3. Confirm the deletion in the confirmation modal.

### Best practices

- **Design for analytics** — Structure your category tree around the dimensions you want to analyse (e.g., by strategy type, by content category, by audience segment).
- **Keep it flat enough** — A deep tree is harder to navigate. Aim for 2-3 levels at most.
- **Name clearly** — Use descriptive names that any team member can understand without context.
